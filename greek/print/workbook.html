Lesson 1: What's in your bag

Week of Oct 26 · This week: έχω (I have)

How to study this page: cover the Greek, say it out loud from the English, then check. Saying it from memory works better than reading it again.

Out in the world this week: Pack a real bag with five things you can name in Greek. Tonight, take each one out and say Έχω ένα… out loud. Write a label by hand for one of them and stick it on.

Today's phrases

Make the gesture every time you say the phrase. It helps the phrase stick.

Phrase Meaning Gesture
Γεια σας Hello (to a group, or polite) Big wave to the whole room, fingers together and slightly bent.
Γεια σου Hi (to one friend) Small wave to one person, fingers together.
Πώς σε λένε; What's your name? Palm-up hand toward the partner, eyebrows up.
Με λένε… My name is… Hand flat on your own chest.
Έχω ένα… I have a… Hold the object up close to your body.
Έχεις ένα…; Do you have a…? Point at the partner's bag, eyebrows up.
Ναι, έχω. Yes, I do. Small Greek nod, head tipping down and to the side, and pat the object.
Τι έχεις; What do you have? Both palms up, shoulders slightly raised.

Main scene

First day: what's in your bag?: Two classmates meet before class and compare what they brought.

New words: σε, η τσάντα, και, ορίστε, ευχαριστώ, παρακαλώ

  • A: Γεια σου! — Hi!
  • B: Γεια σου! Πώς σε λένε; — Hi! What's your name?
  • A: Με λένε Μαρία. Εσένα; — My name is Maria. And you?
  • B: Με λένε Νίκο. — My name is Nikos.
  • A: Τι έχεις στην τσάντα; — What do you have in your bag?
  • B: Έχω ένα βιβλίο και ένα στυλό. Εσύ; — I have a book and a pen. You?
  • A: Έχω ένα κινητό, ένα κλειδί και νερό. — I have a phone, a key, and water.
  • B: Έχεις ένα τετράδιο; — Do you have a notebook?
  • A: Όχι. Έχεις ένα στυλό; — No. Do you have a pen?
  • B: Ναι, έχω. Ορίστε! — Yes, I do. Here you go!
  • A: Ευχαριστώ! — Thank you!
  • B: Παρακαλώ. — You're welcome.
  • A: Γεια σου, Νίκο! — Bye, Nikos!
  • B: Γεια! — Bye!

Replays, swaps, and twists

  • Objects: βιβλίο · στυλό · κινητό · κλειδί · τετράδιο · νερό

  • Retell round: tell the whole scene in Greek to three different partners, with less time each time (60, 45, then 30 seconds). When you're listening, just listen.

Lesson 2: Guest at a Greek home

Week of Nov 2 · This week: singular endings -ω/-εις/-ει

How to study this page: cover the Greek, say it out loud from the English, then check. Saying it from memory works better than reading it again.

Out in the world this week: Offer someone at home a drink in Greek (Θέλεις λίγο νερό;) and let them answer in English. Write the Greek letters you know on a sticky note by the kettle or coffee maker.

Today's phrases

Make the gesture every time you say the phrase. It helps the phrase stick.

Phrase Meaning Gesture
Θέλεις λίγο νερό; Would you like a little water? Offer an imaginary glass with both hands.
Ναι, θέλω λίγο…, ευχαριστώ. Yes, I'd like a little…, thank you. Pinch thumb and finger close together for "a little", then hand to heart.
Όχι, ευχαριστώ. No, thank you. Greek "no": tilt the head up with a small click of the tongue, then hand to heart.
Θέλεις ένα γλυκό; Would you like a sweet? Offer an imaginary spoon of spoon-sweet.
Έχεις ψωμί; Do you have bread? Mime tearing a piece of bread, eyebrows up.
Ο Νίκος θέλει τσάι. Nikos wants tea. Point at "Nikos", then mime holding a hot cup and blowing on it.
…αλλά… …but… One hand out, then the other hand out on the other side.

Main scene

Visiting the neighbor: A guest arrives at a Greek neighbor's house. In Greece a host offers more than once, and a first "no" is expected.

New words: καλώς ήρθες, ο καφές, δεν

  • Host: Γεια σου! Καλώς ήρθες! — Hi! Welcome!
  • Guest: Γεια σου, Μαρία! Ευχαριστώ. — Hi, Maria! Thank you.
  • Host: Θέλεις λίγο νερό; — Would you like a little water?
  • Guest: Ναι, θέλω λίγο νερό, ευχαριστώ. — Yes, I'd like a little water, thank you.
  • Host: Θέλεις τσάι; — Do you want tea?
  • Guest: Ναι, θέλω τσάι, ευχαριστώ. — Yes, I'd like tea, thank you.
  • Host: Έχω και ένα γλυκό. Θέλεις; — I also have a sweet. Would you like one?
  • Guest: Όχι, ευχαριστώ. — No, thank you.
  • Host: Λίγο; Θέλεις λίγο; — A little? Do you want a little?
  • Guest: Ναι, λίγο! Ευχαριστώ. — Yes, a little! Thank you.
  • Host: Νίκο, θέλεις κι εσύ τσάι; — Nikos, do you want tea too?
  • Nikos: Όχι, θέλω καφέ. — No, I want coffee.
  • Host: Ο Νίκος θέλει καφέ, αλλά δεν έχω καφέ! Έχω τσάι. — Nikos wants coffee, but I don't have coffee! I have tea.
  • Nikos: Ναι, τσάι! — Yes, tea!
  • Guest (to the class): Η Μαρία έχει ένα γλυκό. Ο Νίκος θέλει καφέ, αλλά… — Maria has a sweet. Nikos wants coffee, but…

Lesson 3: Making plans

Week of Nov 9 · This week: -ώ stressed verb

How to study this page: cover the Greek, say it out loud from the English, then check. Saying it from memory works better than reading it again.

Out in the world this week: Make one real plan this week (coffee, a walk, a call) and ask for it in Greek first: Μπορείς αύριο; Write the plan on a paper calendar in Greek.

Today's phrases

Make the gesture every time you say the phrase. It helps the phrase stick.

Phrase Meaning Gesture
Μπορώ; May I? Reach toward an object and pause, eyebrows up.
Ναι, βέβαια. Yes, of course. Big open-handed "go ahead" sweep.
Μπορείς; Can you? Point at the partner and tap your wrist.
Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ. I want to, but I can't. Reach out eagerly, then pull back and shake your head.
Μπορείς σήμερα το βράδυ; Can you make it tonight? Point down at the floor ("today"), then lay your head on your hands ("evening").
Λυπάμαι, δεν μπορώ. Sorry, I can't. Hand on chest with a sad face, then a small head shake.
Αύριο το πρωί μπορώ. Tomorrow morning I can. Roll a hand forward ("tomorrow"), then stretch both arms up as if waking.
Έχεις ώρα; Do you have time? Tap your wrist.

Main scene

Can you make it?: Two friends ask permission and try to find a time to meet.

New words: γιατί, τέλεια, καλά, εμείς, εντάξει

  • A: Γεια σου, Νίκο! Μπορώ; (points to the empty chair) — Hi, Nikos! May I?
  • B: Ναι, βέβαια! — Yes, of course!
  • A: Έχεις ώρα σήμερα; — Do you have time today?
  • B: Όχι σήμερα. Γιατί; — Not today. Why?
  • A: Θέλω καφέ. Μπορείς σήμερα το βράδυ; — I want a coffee. Can you make it tonight?
  • B: Λυπάμαι, δεν μπορώ. Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ. — Sorry, I can't. I want to, but I can't.
  • A: Αύριο το πρωί; — Tomorrow morning?
  • B: Αύριο το πρωί… ναι, μπορώ! — Tomorrow morning… yes, I can!
  • A: Τέλεια! Και η Μαρία; Μπορεί; — Perfect! And Maria? Can she?
  • B: Η Μαρία δεν μπορεί το πρωί. Μπορεί το βράδυ. — Maria can't in the morning. She can in the evening.
  • A: Καλά. Αύριο το πρωί εμείς, και το βράδυ η Μαρία! — Okay. Tomorrow morning us, and in the evening Maria!
  • B: Εντάξει! Γεια! — Okay! Bye!

Lesson 4: Postcards and the cafe

Week of Nov 16 · This week: Τι…; + το/τον/τη

How to study this page: cover the Greek, say it out loud from the English, then check. Saying it from memory works better than reading it again.

Today's phrases

Make the gesture every time you say the phrase. It helps the phrase stick.

Phrase Meaning Gesture

Μπορείς τώρα; Can you now? Point at the floor twice, quickly ("now").

Main scene

Capstone: three scenes: Students perform three short scenes (café, plans, market), then celebrate the year.

New words: μπακλαβά

  • Waiter: Καλημέρα! Τι θέλετε; — Good morning! What would you like?
  • A: Έναν καφέ και ένα νερό, παρακαλώ. — A coffee and a water, please.
  • Waiter: Τίποτα άλλο; — Anything else?
  • A: Έχετε γλυκό; — Do you have anything sweet?
  • Waiter: Ναι, έχουμε μπακλαβά. — Yes, we have baklava.
  • A: Ένα, παρακαλώ. — One, please.
  • B: Τι θα κάνεις το Σαββατοκύριακο; — What will you do this weekend?
  • A: Θα πάω στη θάλασσα. Θέλεις να έρθεις; — I'll go to the beach. Do you want to come?
  • B: Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ το Σάββατο. Έχω δουλειά. Την Κυριακή μπορώ. — I'd like to, but I can't on Saturday. I have work. I can on Sunday.
  • A: Τέλεια, την Κυριακή στις δέκα! — Perfect, Sunday at ten!
  • A: Πόσο κάνουν οι ελιές; — How much are the olives?
  • Seller: Πέντε ευρώ το κιλό. — Five euros a kilo.
  • A: Μισό κιλό, παρακαλώ. — Half a kilo, please.
  • Seller: Ορίστε. Τίποτα άλλο; — Here you go. Anything else?
  • A: Όχι, ευχαριστώ. Καλή σας μέρα! — No, thank you. Have a good day!
  • Teacher: Μπράβο σε όλους! Καλό καλοκαίρι! — Well done, everyone! Have a great summer!
  • Everyone: Καλό καλοκαίρι! — Have a great summer!

Practice

Replays, swaps, and twists

Replay the scene with a partner, with the page face-down. Peek only when you get stuck. Change one thing each time.

Swap one thing

  • Café: καφέ · τσάι · νερό · γλυκό
  • Plans: θα πάω… · θα δω… · θα φάμε…
  • Market: ελιές · ντομάτες · ένα κιλό · μισό κιλό
  • An old line: work in one review phrase, for example Χριστός Ανέστη! – Αληθώς Ανέστη!

Every time

  • Trade parts with your partner.
  • Tell it about someone else (έχει, έχουμε, έχουν).
  • Tell it as yesterday (ήθελα, πήγα…) or as tomorrow (θα…).
  • Retell round: tell the whole scene in Greek to three different partners, with less time each time (2 minutes, 90 seconds, then 1 minute). When you're listening, just listen.

Build it with tiles

Cut out your Lesson 26 tiles (shaded tiles are verbs). Lay out the sentence mat, then for each sentence:

  1. Build it from the English, then read it aloud.
  2. Turn it into a question: swap the . for ; and raise your voice at the end.
  3. Make it negative: put δεν right before the verb.
  4. Swap one tile for another word from the extra tiles.
  5. Write your last version by hand in your notebook.
